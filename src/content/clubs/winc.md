**What it is:** a wine club that quizzes your taste (six questions), matches you with bottles, and refines the picks as you rate what you drink. You can swap bottles before a shipment goes out — three days before, they email you the lineup.

## What happened to Bright Cellars?

If you're here because you searched for Bright Cellars: it's a Winc membership now. Bright Cellars customers were moved over in **October 2024**, accounts transferred automatically, and `brightcellars.com` now sends you to a Winc activation page. Same schedule, similar perks — free shipping, skip anytime, satisfaction guarantee — plus the 20% member discount. Nothing for you to do if you were already a member; if you weren't, everything below applies to you as a new one.

## The math (the only part that matters)

| | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Intro offer | **$100 off over your first 4 orders** | winc.com (checked Oct 16, 2026) |
| Typical order | 6-pack ≈ **$115** · 12-pack ≈ **$220** | Winc's own average, after discount |

| Member discount | **20% off everything, always** | visible in listed prices (e.g. $16.80 vs $21 reg.) |

The math to run: **would you pay $16–20 a bottle at the store for wines someone else picked?** If yes, Winc is a convenience play with a strong first year — the $100 intro carries the first four orders, then you're at member pricing. If you're a $8 supermarket drinker, this is not your club — genuinely.

## What the terms of service add (the part the banner leaves out)

Two claims on the membership page and the same two claims in the terms — except the terms say something different:

1. **"Free Shipping always" vs. "A shipping charge of $9 may apply."** The banner is unambiguous. The terms of service read: *"A shipping charge of $9 may apply based on the size of your order."* Both sentences are Winc's. Which one shows up on your invoice depends on your order size.
2. **The 3-day rule.** *"Please notify us at least 3 days prior to any shipment to update your account information, payment method, change your address, skip a shipment, or to cancel your membership."* Skipping is self-service — but "self-service" still has a deadline, and one full business day of slack is not the same as "anytime".
3. **Watch the second product.** *Winc Insider Access* enrols you automatically when you buy anything and opt in, and then charges **$59.95 a month** as member credits unless you skip at least three days before your enrolment date. The credits are real money — but member-credit purchases are *"non-refundable and not cancellable"* per the same terms.

None of this is buried in a dark corner: it's on winc.com, in the terms of service. It just isn't on the page with the wine photos.

## The fine print we read

- **No commitment, per their site.** Skip, pause, or cancel anytime; swaps before shipping are self-service.
- **Free shipping on subscription orders** — no shipping minimums on member deliveries.
- **The $100 comes over 4 orders, not all at once.** Read that twice. It's still a good deal; it's just not a "free wine" coupon.
- **State rules apply.** Wine shipping is regulated; select your state and see before you fall for a bottle.
- **Someone 21+ signs at the door.** Plan for it.

## Cancelling Winc (the 3-day line)

Winc is in the easier half: **skip, pause or cancel in your account**, self-service, per their terms. No phone call required.

The catch is timing, not friction. Their terms require notice **at least 3 days before a shipment goes out**. Miss that window and the box is already moving — you'll be paying for a shipment you meant to stop. Two practical consequences:

1. **Put the date in your calendar**, not your head. The 3-day line moves silently with every shipment.
2. **Decide before the box, not after.** Once it ships, the question stops being "can I cancel" and becomes "can I return it" — a different conversation with a different answer.

One contradiction we found while reading: their terms include a line about a **"$9 [fee] may apply"** while the site elsewhere promises free shipping always. We quote both rather than pick the friendlier one. If you're cancelling and a fee appears that you can't map to a clause, ask them which paragraph it comes from.

**We have not tested this flow.** What we can say is what their paperwork says: self-service, 3-day notice, no tie-in.

If you'd rather join a club where leaving is a click with no window, [Firstleaf](/wine-clubs/firstleaf/) is the same self-service model without the 3-day rule — see [Firstleaf vs Winc](/guides/firstleaf-vs-winc/).

## Who it's for

- You drink $15–20 bottles, like trying unfamiliar labels, and won't miss picking every bottle yourself.
- You want the "quiz → rate → refine" loop to do the shopping for you.
- You were a Bright Cellars member and want to know what you're in now.

## Who it's not for

- You're loyal to the $8 supermarket shelf. Stay there.
- You want famous-name producers. Winc leans on its own labels.
- You want to hand-pick every single bottle (swaps exist, but the base is curated).
