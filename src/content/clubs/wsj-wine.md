**What it is:** a licensing arrangement, and it's worth being precise about it. WSJ Wine is **managed by Direct Wines, Inc.**, and its own footer says it is *"operated independently of The Wall Street Journal and Dow Jones & Company, Inc."* The newspaper lends its name; Direct Wines — a large direct-to-consumer wine operation — runs the club and the fulfilment through licensed retailers.

So don't read this as a newspaper's tasting judgement. Read it as Direct Wines with a familiar logo. That's not a scandal, but it changes what the brand name is worth.

## The math

The number that matters is buried in the checkout consent, not on the homepage:

> "…receive a new 12-bottle selection every 3 months and will soon be eligible for 2 extra-special seasonal cases, each only **$184.99 plus $19.99 shipping and tax**."

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Seasonal case, 12 bottles | $184.99 |
| Shipping | $19.99 |
| **Before tax** | **$204.98** |
| **Per bottle** | **~$17.08** |

Add tax and it drifts upward. For $17 a bottle you're in Winc or Firstleaf territory — clubs that also let you see what's coming and skip online. WSJ Wine's difference is curation and the newspaper-adjacent framing, not price.

## The fine print we read

- **10 days' notice** before the next charge, if you want to skip, change or delay — "online or by phone", per the consent text.
- **20% off future club cases** once subscribed.
- **100% satisfaction guarantee** — stated, without a described process for claiming it.
- **Free items on orders of $199+** on the shop side, which sits just under the $204.98 case total.
- **1–3 business days** typical delivery.
- **Licensed retailer fulfilment** in your state — so availability and tax follow your address, not the brand.

## Cancelling WSJ Wine (one number, no button)

The cancellation route is stated in the same consent paragraph that contains the prices: **"I can also cancel my membership anytime at 1-877-975-9463."**

That's the whole documented procedure — a phone number. No account-page cancel button appears in their FAQ navigation, which lists Account Questions but not a self-service exit.

Two things follow. First, the *right* to cancel is contractual and clear (anytime, their words). Second, the *act* of cancelling is a phone call during business hours, which is friction by design — not villainy, but not nothing.

**We have not made that call.** We can't tell you how long it takes, whether they offer a retention discount, or whether cancelling mid-cycle refunds anything. If any of that matters to you, the 10-day notice window is your safety margin: start the call on day one of it, not day nine.

## Who it's for

- You want a **curated** case and don't want to pick bottles yourself.
- $17 a bottle is your normal spend, not your splurge.
- You're comfortable making one phone call to leave a subscription.
- The "WSJ" name genuinely reassures you — fine, just know who's actually pouring.

## Who it's not for

- You want click-to-cancel. Plonk and Firstleaf both document one; WSJ Wine doesn't.
- You're hunting the cheapest bottle — $17.08 is not that, and neither is $19.99 shipping.
- You'd feel misled later to learn the newspaper isn't involved. Better to know now.
