Wine clubs aren't a scam. They're just built on one simple trick: **the first box is priced to make you say yes, and every box after that is priced to make money.** Neither is evil — but if you only look at the intro price, you're not shopping, you're being shopped.

Here's the checklist we run before joining anything. Five minutes, saves real money.

## Trap 1: The intro price is not the price

Always do one calculation before you click: **price per bottle after the intro.** Not the intro — after.

Real example (checked Oct 16, 2026):

- Firstleaf: first box $44.95 for 6 bottles → ~**$7.50/bottle**
- Every shipment after: $99 for 6 bottles → ~**$16.50/bottle**

That's not a hidden fee — it's stated. But the jump from $7.50 to $16.50 is where the economics live. The club can still be worth it at $16.50 — just *decide* at $16.50, not at $7.50.

**Rule:** divide the ongoing price by the bottle count, then ask yourself: "would I buy this bottle at the store for this?" If the answer is no, the club is entertainment, not a deal.

## Trap 2: The fine print that isn't on the landing page

Three things decide whether a club works for you — and none of them are in the big headline:

1. **Shipping states.** Wine is state-regulated in the US. Plenty of clubs can't ship to your state. Check *before* you enter your card, not after.
2. **Adult signature.** Someone 21+ must sign at the door. If you're never home, your wine sits at a depot.
3. **The guarantee terms.** "Money back" and "we'll credit your next box" are different things. Read which one it is.

## Trap 3: "No commitment" is a claim, not a fact

Every club says cancel anytime. Nobody says *how*. Before you order, know these three:

- Where's the cancel button? (Account settings? Email? Phone?)
- What happens to a shipment that's already in motion?
- Can you pause instead of cancel?

Screenshot the terms page **before** you sign up. Takes 20 seconds, ends every future argument.

## The 5-minute pre-join checklist

1. Price per bottle **after** the intro → written down
2. Ships to my state? → checked at checkout
3. How do I cancel? → found the button/email/phone first
4. Guarantee: refund or credit? → known
5. Am I willing to rate bottles? → if no, skip personalized clubs entirely

## Our verdict on clubs in general

Clubs are worth it if **all three** are true: you drink $12–20 bottles anyway, you like variety more than control, and you'll give the algorithm feedback. Then it's a convenience play that pays for itself.

Skip them if you're happy with supermarket wine (genuinely — stay there), love picking your own bottles, or won't rate anything. A personalized club you don't give feedback to is just a random wine box with a subscription.
