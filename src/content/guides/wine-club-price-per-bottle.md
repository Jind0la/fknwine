Every wine club leads with the same trick: an intro price that makes the first box look like a steal. It often is. The problem is the box *after* it — and the one after that.

So we did the boring thing and put the real numbers in one table. **Price per bottle, after any intro, before shipping**, for the six clubs we've reviewed. All figures from the companies' own sites, checked **October 16, 2026**.

## The table

| Club | Headline | What you actually pay per bottle | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Firstleaf** | $44.95 for 6 bottles | **~$7.50** intro → **~$16.50** ongoing | $99 per 6-bottle shipment after the intro |
| **Winc** | $100 off your first 4 orders | **~$16–20** at member pricing | always-on 20% member discount |
| **The California Wine Club** | from $49 per shipment | **~$24.50** | 2 bottles at base; shipping extra |
| **Gold Medal Wine Club** | from $49 per shipment | **~$24.50+** | Gold 2-bottle base; shipping extra, scales with tier |
| **Wine Access** | $150 per shipment | **~$25** (Discovery) → **~$75** (Connoisseurs) | Discovery is 6 bottles; Connoisseurs is 2 for the same $150 |
| **Naked Wines** | $40/month + up to 60% off | *member price, spent from your $40/month wallet* | not a per-shipment club — you fund an account |

Two clubs sit under $20 a bottle. Four sit at $24 and up. That gap is the entire decision.

## Why the headline lies (and doesn't)

The headline isn't dishonest — it's *incomplete*. Three things push the real number above it:

1. **The intro ends.** Firstleaf's $7.50 a bottle is real, once. Every box after is $16.50. The jump between those two numbers is where the club makes its money.
2. **Shipping isn't in the headline.** Gold Medal and The California Wine Club bill shipping separately; it varies by state and bottle count. A $24.50 bottle can land at $28+.
3. **Tiers reprice everything.** Wine Access's Discovery is $25 a bottle; its Connoisseurs club is the *same $150 a shipment* for two bottles — $75 each. Same retailer, triple the per-bottle cost, one click apart.

## How to use this

- **Know your real ceiling.** Divide the ongoing (non-intro) price by the bottles. Ask: would I buy this bottle at the store for that? If no, you're buying entertainment, not value — fine, just name it.
- **Compare landed, not headline.** Add the shipping rule before you compare two clubs. Shipping-equal-but-free-at-12-bottles changes the ranking.
- **Treat the intro as a trial, not a price.** The fair question isn't "is $7.50 a bottle good?" — it's "is $16.50 a bottle good, after I've tasted the first box?"
- **Match the club to the night.** Weeknight wine lives at $8–20 a bottle. If your table is a $25+ table, the discovery clubs (CWC, Gold Medal, Wine Access) make sense. If it isn't, they don't — and that's fine.

## The honest caveats

- **A $25 club bottle ≠ a $25 supermarket bottle.** Curated small-producer wine and mass-market shelf wine aren't the same product. Price isn't the only axis — sourcing is the other one.
- **Naked Wines doesn't fit the table.** It's a prepaid wallet, not a per-shipment price, so we won't fake a per-bottle number for it. Its math is a rhythm question, not a bottle question.
- **Prices move.** Everything here carries a check date for a reason. We re-read the clubs' pages and update the dates; if a number looks stale, it probably is, and we'll fix it.
